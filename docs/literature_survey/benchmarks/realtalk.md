# RealTalk: A 21-Day Real-World Dataset for Long-Term Conversation

## 0. Plain-English TL;DR (What It Tests & Why We Use It)

- **What This Benchmark Is:**
  RealTalk is a 21-day conversational dataset containing authentic, unscripted text messaging exchanges between real human pairs on mobile chat apps. Spanning 10 distinct long-term conversations with roughly 21 daily sessions and over 17,000 tokens per dialogue, it captures genuine human conversational habits: erratic response delays ranging from minutes to over 27 hours, rapid bursts of consecutive short chat bubbles, personal disclosures, slang, typos, and emotional bonding that evolves over weeks.

- **Why Standard Tests Aren't Enough:**
  - **Synthetic Chats Are Too Polite and Predictable:** Datasets generated by having two large language models talk to each other (such as LoCoMo) suffer from artificial patterns: they start with unrealistically high intimacy right away, shower each other with excessive empathy even when discussing neutral or negative topics, and always exchange neat, single-sentence turns.
  - **Needle-In-A-Haystack Tests Ignore Real Dialogue Dynamics:** Standard synthetic retrieval tests hide a single artificial sentence inside a book or essay. They completely fail to test whether an assistant can track conversational threads that pause overnight, handle five rapid-fire chat bubbles sent in a single minute, or recall personal facts shared casually three weeks ago across 20 distinct sessions.
  - **Summary Memory Hides Catastrophic Failures:** Many commercial systems attempt to save memory by summarizing past chats into short bullet points or event lists. RealTalk's evaluation proves that summarizing chats destroys multi-hop reasoning by over 40% to 46%, because bullet points strip away the subtle circumstantial details needed to connect separate clues across time.

- **How It Works Step-by-Step:**
  1. *Authentic Chat Collection:* Ten human participants were paired across 10 long-term chat threads on WhatsApp, chatting daily for 16 to 21 days with at least 50 messages per day, exchanging stories, daily news, personal updates, and shared images (which are converted to descriptive image captions).
  2. *Persona Simulation Task:* A model is given the conversation history and asked to generate the next response on behalf of a specific user. The response is evaluated to see if the model successfully captures that user's unique tone, vocabulary, reflectiveness, empathy, and social engagement style.
  3. *Long-Term Memory Probing Task:* Models are tested on 728 human-annotated questions targeting facts mentioned throughout the 21 days. Questions are split into three difficulty types: multi-hop reasoning (combining clues from multiple separate days), temporal reasoning (figuring out when events occurred based on dates and relative phrasing like "last Friday"), and commonsense dialogue understanding.

## 1. Key Takeaways & How We Use It in `kv_bench`

- **Specific KV-Cache Failures Exposed:**
  - **The Summary Memory Fallacy:** RealTalk demonstrates that replacing raw conversational tokens with high-level summary statements or event lists causes GPT-4o's multi-hop reasoning accuracy to plummet from 51.9% to 26.6% (a ~49% relative collapse). For our project, this is definitive empirical proof that naive summarization cannot replace a true KV cache. To preserve reasoning, the system must retain actual token states in tiered memory (GPU VRAM + host CPU RAM).
  - **Ragged, Multi-Bubble Burst Ingestion:** Real users do not alternate neatly turn-by-turn. RealTalk users sent an average of 2.31 consecutive messages per turn, with occasional bursts reaching up to 68 consecutive chat bubbles. KV cache systems that assume clean alternating dialogue boundaries experience memory fragmentation and inefficient prefill chunking unless block allocation handles ragged multi-bubble bursts gracefully.
  - **Context Dilution in Persona Mimicry:** The paper finds that simply feeding longer raw conversation history into an LLM's prompt fails to improve persona or style mimicry; style accuracy saturates after just 3 sessions. Without targeted selective retention or fine-tuning, stuffing tens of thousands of tokens of past chit-chat into the cache wastes precious VRAM without improving response personalization.

- **Key Findings from the Paper Worth Knowing:**
  1. **Event Condensation Destroys Multi-Hop Connections:** While event-based memory summaries reduce token count, they wipe out the conversational context and implicit links between turns. Multi-hop questions suffer the steepest accuracy penalty (41% to 46% drop in F1), whereas single-point temporal questions suffer significantly less.
  2. **Human Intimacy Builds Slowly; LLMs Hallucinate Instant Closeness:** Real human chatters start with low intimacy and gradually build rapport across 21 days, exhibiting high emotional diversity and variable empathy depending on the topic. Synthetic LLM dialogues start at peak intimacy on Day 1 and exhibit uniform, excessive empathy that feels robotic.
  3. **Style Satures Rapidly Across History:** Giving a model more than 3 sessions of prior dialogue history yields zero improvement in mimicking a speaker's emotional tone or vocabulary. In contrast, lightweight fine-tuning on a user's prior messages dramatically boosts emotional alignment and grounding frequency without requiring massive context windows.

- **Verification of Team Notes (`manual_reviews.md`):**

  - *Correction & Refinement from PDF:* The team note suggested that RealTalk only performs "a simple information retrieval which is not as advanced as others." The PDF proves this impression was incorrect: RealTalk includes a comprehensive 728-question diagnostic memory probe specifically broken down into Multi-Hop, Temporal Reasoning, and Commonsense categories. More importantly, its experimental comparison between full context ($C$) and event memory ($E$) provides one of the strongest published arguments for our project's tiered KV caching over LLM summarization APIs.

- **Task 1: Persona Simulation & Emotional Intelligence (EI) Formulation:**
  A conversation $C = \{S_1, S_2, \dots, S_m\}$ contains sessions $S_i = \{M_{s_1, 1}, M_{s_2, 2}, \dots, M_{s_n, n}\}$ from speakers $s_j \in \{1, 2\}$. Consecutive messages from the same speaker are concatenated prior to analysis. The model predicts the next message $\hat{M}_t$ given history $H_t = \{M_1, \dots, M_{t-1}\}$.

  1. *Session Average Intimacy:*
     $$\text{intimacy\_average}(s, S_i) = \frac{\sum_{M \in M_s^{S_i}} I(M)}{|M_s^{S_i}|}$$
     *Plain-English translation:* The intimacy score $I(M)$ of each individual message sent by speaker $s$ during session $S_i$ is summed up and divided by the total number of messages $|M_s^{S_i}|$ that speaker sent in that session.

  2. *Intimacy Progression (Linear & Exponential Fits):*
     $$\text{linear\_progression}(s) = a, \quad \text{where } y = a \cdot x + b$$
     $$\text{exp\_progression}(s) = b, \quad \text{where } y = a \cdot e^{b \cdot x}$$
     *Plain-English translation:* The slope $a$ from linear regression or exponent $b$ from exponential regression of session intimacy scores across time $x \in \{1, \dots, m\}$, measuring whether intimacy grows or stalls.

  3. *Reflective Frequency:*
     $$\text{reflective\_frequency}(s) = \frac{\sum_{M \in M_s} R(M)}{|M_s|}$$
     *Plain-English translation:* The proportion of messages sent by speaker $s$ where the boolean indicator $R(M) = 1$, indicating the speaker explicitly reflected on their own thoughts, feelings, or inner reactions.

  4. *Sentiment and Emotion Diversity (Shannon Entropy):*
     $$\text{sentiment\_diversity}(s) = - \sum_{x \in \mathcal{L}} p_s(x) \log_2 p_s(x), \quad \text{where } p_s(x) = \frac{\sum_{M \in M_s} \mathbf{1}(S(M) = x)}{|M_s|}$$
     *Plain-English translation:* The entropy of the distribution of sentiment labels $x \in \mathcal{L} = \{\text{positive}, \text{negative}, \text{neutral}\}$ across all messages of speaker $s$. Higher values indicate a wider emotional range rather than uniform positivity. (The same equation defines $\text{emotion\_diversity}$ across 11 discrete emotion categories).

  5. *Emotion & Sentiment Stability (Consecutive Persistence):*
     $$\text{stability}(s) = \frac{\sum_{k=2}^{|M_s|} \mathbf{1}\left(E(M_s^{(k)}) = E(M_s^{(k-1)})\right)}{|M_s| - 1}$$
     *Plain-English translation:* The proportion of consecutive messages from speaker $s$ that maintain the exact same emotion label $E(M)$, measuring emotional inertia versus erratic switching.

  6. *Emotion & Sentiment Alignment (Partner Synchronization):*
     $$\text{alignment}(s) = \frac{\sum_k \mathbf{1}\left(E(M_s^{(k)}) = E(M_p^{(k)})\right)}{|M_s|}$$
     *Plain-English translation:* The frequency with which speaker $s$'s emotion matches their partner $p$'s corresponding emotion, capturing emotional attunement.

  7. *Motivation / Grounding Frequency:*
     $$\text{grounding\_frequency}(s) = \frac{\sum_{M \in M_s} G(M)}{|M_s|}$$
     *Plain-English translation:* The fraction of messages where boolean indicator $G(M) = 1$, indicating a grounding act (such as follow-up clarifying questions or confirmation checks).

  8. *Empathy Score (EPITOME Framework):*
     $$\text{empathy}(s) = \frac{\sum_{M \in M_s} \text{EP}(M)}{|M_s|}$$
     $$\text{EP}(M) = \text{Emotional Reaction} + \text{Interpretation} + \text{Exploration} \in [0, 6]$$
     *Plain-English translation:* Average score of 3 sub-components evaluated on a $0\text{--}2$ Likert scale by an LLM evaluator according to the EPITOME standard.

## 3. Role in Senior Project ([`docs/proposal.md`](../../proposal.md) Section 3.3.2)

- **Role:** **Final Report Metric & Real-World Conversational Stress Test (`WP-7`)**
  - Serves as our primary real-world conversational evaluation workload to benchmark multi-session chat retention in `WP-7`.
  - While `LongMemEval` tests structured synthetic personal memory skills (IE, KU, TR, ABS), `RealTalk` tests unstructured, colloquial human messaging containing typos, slang, asynchronous time gaps, and bursty chat bubbles.
  - Directly validates our core architectural thesis: **preserving compressed/tiered raw KV tokens in CPU RAM beats condensing conversations into event summaries by over $40\%\text{--}50\%$ in multi-hop accuracy**.

- **Consumer Hardware KV Footprint Analysis (8 GB RTX 4060 & 16 GB RTX 4090 Cap):**
  - Using our project's pinned Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) formula from [`docs/MODELS.md`](../../MODELS.md):
    $$\text{KV Bytes per Token} = 4 \times L \times H_{KV} \times D_{\text{head}}$$
    $$\text{KV Footprint (GB)} = \frac{\text{KV Bytes per Token} \times S \times N_{\text{users}}}{1024^3}$$
  - For a full RealTalk conversation ($S \approx 17,110\text{ tokens}$):
    - **Qwen 3 4B Instruct** ($L=36, H_{KV}=8, D=128$, $144\text{ KiB/token}$):
      - Single conversation: $144\text{ KiB} \times 17,110 \approx \mathbf{2.41\text{ GB}}$ of KV cache in native BF16.
      - 10 concurrent SME user sessions: $10 \times 2.41\text{ GB} = \mathbf{24.10\text{ GB}}$ of KV cache!
      - Adding unquantized model weights ($8.06\text{ GB}$): Total VRAM required $= \mathbf{32.16\text{ GB}}$.
    - **Phi-4 mini Instruct** ($L=32, H_{KV}=8, D=128$, $128\text{ KiB/token}$):
      - Single conversation: $128\text{ KiB} \times 17,110 \approx \mathbf{2.14\text{ GB}}$ of KV cache in native BF16.
      - 10 concurrent SME user sessions: $10 \times 2.14\text{ GB} = \mathbf{21.40\text{ GB}}$ of KV cache.
      - Adding unquantized model weights ($7.65\text{ GB}$): Total VRAM required $= \mathbf{29.05\text{ GB}}$.

  - **The Hardware Memory Wall:**
    - On an **8 GB RTX 4060**, Qwen 3 4B weights ($8.06\text{ GB}$) consume $100\%$ of VRAM before caching a single token, causing an immediate Out-Of-Memory (OOM) crash.
    - On a **16 GB RTX 4090 cap**, available KV headroom is $\approx 7.94\text{ GB}$. While a single user's 17K conversation fits ($2.41\text{ GB}$), running 4 or more concurrent conversations immediately triggers an OOM crash.
    - Under our **3-axis co-optimization** (priority scoring + selective 4-bit quantization + PCIe host offloading to 16 GB system RAM):
      - High-priority tokens ($20\%$) stay in GPU VRAM in BF16: $0.20 \times 24.1\text{ GB} = 4.82\text{ GB}$.
      - Evicted low-priority tokens ($80\%$) are quantized to 4-bit ($4\times$ reduction) and staged in host RAM: $0.80 \times 24.1 / 4 = 4.82\text{ GB}$ of host RAM.
      - Total GPU VRAM required: $8.06\text{ GB (weights)} + 4.82\text{ GB (KV)} = \mathbf{12.88\text{ GB}}$, comfortably fitting within our $16\text{ GB}$ hardware ceiling while serving all 10 users!

- **Execution Strategy in `kv_bench`:**
  1. *Trace Replay Harness:* Replay RealTalk's timestamped dialogues to simulate asynchronous multi-user chat sessions, injecting realistic inter-message idle gaps and multi-bubble bursts to test PagedAttention block allocation.
  2. *Context Sweeps:* Scale conversation prefixes across standardized evaluation points ($4\text{K}, 8\text{K}, 12\text{K},$ and full $17.1\text{K}$ tokens).
  3. *Accuracy Benchmark:* Measure Partial Match F1 and LLM Accuracy across the 728 memory probing questions (Multi-Hop, Temporal Reasoning, Commonsense) comparing our co-optimized engine against native uncompressed vLLM baselines to verify that selective quantization and offloading do not incur the devastating 41–46% penalty seen in summary-based memory.
